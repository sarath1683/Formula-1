    Chapter 1

    Historical background

    A car is always close to the ground, which makes its airflow harder to understand than an aircraft's. Both fight drag, but where a plane needs lift, a racing car wants the opposite: downforce, which presses the tyres into the track so they can accelerate, brake and corner harder.

    Why downforce changed everything

    The chapter's central claim is that downforce "clearly adds to the tyres' grip". This simplified model shows how much. Tyre grip is roughly friction coefficient times the load pressing down on the tyres. Aerodynamic force grows with the square of speed, so the faster the car goes, the more the air helps (or hurts).

    F = ½ · ρ · V² · (CL·A)

    Model: 700 kg car, tyre friction μ = 1.6, air density ρ = 1.225 kg/m³. CL·A is lift coefficient times area; negative means downforce. Real cars vary, and Chapter 2 covers the equation properly.
